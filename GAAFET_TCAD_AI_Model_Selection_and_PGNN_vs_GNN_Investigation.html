Graph Neural Networks (GNN) vs. Physics-Guided NNs (PGNN)

To give you a rigorous justification to present to your supervisor and team, here is a detailed breakdown of the merits, demerits, and architectural fit of GNN vs. PGNN for TCAD GAAFET modeling.

Graph Neural Network (GNN)
GNN

GNNs operate on graph structures $G=(V, E)$ where nodes represent TCAD mesh vertices (x,y,z coordinates) and edges represent spatial connections.

Merits:
  • Predicts full 3D spatial fields (potential, electron density, lattice temperature maps).
  • Handles non-Euclidean, irregular finite-element meshes seamlessly.
  • Geometry-agnostic: Can adapt to structural topology changes without retraining.
Demerits:
  • Severe Over-Engineering: You need terminal PPA scalars ($I_{\text{ON}}, I_{\text{OFF}}, C_{\text{gg}}$), not 500,000 mesh node values.
  • Huge Data & Memory Overhead: Requires full 3D TCAD spatial mesh dumps (gigabytes per run).
  • No Physics Guarantees: Pure GNN message passing can violate current continuity & conservation laws.
  • Incompatible with SPICE: Cannot be directly compiled into Verilog-A analytical functions.
Physics-Guided Neural Network (PGNN / PKID-ANN) ★
PGNN

PGNN embeds physics equations (analytical GAA models, subthreshold electrostatics, drift-diffusion constraints) into residual learning or network loss functions.

Merits:
  • Sample Efficiency: Learns residual $e = y_{\text{TCAD}} - y_{\text{LF}}$, reaching target accuracy with 60-80% fewer TCAD runs.
  • Physical Consistency: Eliminates unphysical oscillations, non-monotonic $I_D$, or negative capacitances.
  • Parametric PPA Optimization: Inputs are exact design knobs ($L_g, W_{\text{ns}}, T_{\text{ns}}, N_{\text{sd}}$).
  • SPICE Verilog-A Ready: Easily exported for circuit-level ring oscillator & amplifier simulations.
Demerits:
  • Requires selecting/implementing a suitable Low-Fidelity (LF) baseline model.
  • Does not predict internal 3D spatial field distributions (focuses on terminal I-V/C-V).

5 Key Strategies to Make Your PGNN Model Superior

To make your project publishable and outperform published benchmarks (Xu 2022, Patel 2025, Ehteshamuddin 2024), implement these five targeted enhancements:

1. Formulate a Robust Low-Fidelity (LF) Baseline
1

Use simplified continuous analytical equations for 3D GAAFET electrostatics (e.g. core BSIM-CMG subthreshold equation or parabolic potential approximation). When the LF model captures the exponential $I_{\text{sub}}$ trend and linear $I_{\text{lin}}$ region, your PGNN will converge in under 1,500 TCAD samples.

2. Add Physics Loss Regularization Penalties
2

Incorporate domain physics directly into the loss function during backpropagation:

Loss = MSE(y_pred, y_tcad) + λ1 * ReLU(60 mV/dec - SS_pred) # SS cannot violate thermionic limit (60mV/dec) + λ2 * ReLU(-∂Id/∂Vgs) # Monotonicity: Id must rise with Vgs + λ3 * ReLU(-∂Id/∂Vds) # Output conductance gd must remain non-negative
3. Adaptive Active Sampling (Bayesian Optimization)
3

Do not rely solely on static Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS). Use an ensemble of 5 PGNN models to measure prediction variance (uncertainty). Run Sentaurus TCAD simulations only where model uncertainty is highest or near the Pareto boundary. This cuts required TCAD runs by another 50%!

4. Multi-Task Output Head for DC, RF, & Noise
4

Structure your PGNN with a single shared representation trunk and distinct heads for $I_{\text{D}}$, $C_{\text{gg}}$, transconductance ($g_m = \partial I_D/\partial V_{GS}$), and output conductance ($g_d = \partial I_D/\partial V_{DS}$). Predicting derivatives jointly prevents numerical noise during RF $f_T$ evaluation.

5. Verilog-A Compact Model & SPICE Circuit Co-Optimization
5

Convert the trained PGNN weights into a SPICE-compatible Verilog-A script (as done in Ehteshamuddin 2024). Run circuit simulations for a 17-stage Ring Oscillator (RO) and a Common-Source (CS) RF Amplifier. This bridges device-level physics with system-level PPA performance, making your project paper robust and high-impact.
